Free Algebras — Concise Notes

1. Words and Free Monoid

Let X = {xi | i ∈ I} be a nonempty (possibly indexed) set.

Word over X: a finite sequence of elements from X, written as xi1 xi2 ··· xim. The empty word is denoted by 1 and has length 0.

Define multiplication by juxtaposition: 

(x_{i1} ··· x_{im}) · (x_{j1} ··· x_{jn}) := x_{i1} ··· x_{im} x_{j1} ··· x_{jn}.
With this multiplication, the set of all words forms a monoid (identity 1), denoted X*, called the free monoid on X.

Each element w ∈ X* (i.e., a word) can be uniquely written as 

w = x_{i1}^{k1} x_{i2}^{k2} ··· x_{ir}^{kr},
      where x_{ij} ∈ X, k_j ∈ ℕ, and x_{ij} ≠ x_{i(j+1)}.

Equality in X*: Two words w, w' are equal iff they contain the same sequence of symbols in the same order.

2. Free Algebra

Let F be a field. The free algebra on X over F, denoted F<X>, is the monoid algebra F[X*].

Elements: An element f ∈ F<X> is a finite linear combination of words: 

f = \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i w_i,
      with \lambda_i ∈ F, \; w_i ∈ X*.
Two such sums are equal if, after combining like terms (words equal in X*), their coefficients agree.

Operations:

  • Addition: combine like monomials (words).
  • Scalar multiplication: for λ ∈ F, λ f = \sum λ λ_i w_i.
  • Multiplication: extend bilinearly via 
    (λ w)(μ v) = (λ μ) (w v)
    where w v denotes concatenation in X*.

Algebra axioms: F<X> is an associative unital algebra over F, satisfying associativity, distributivity, scalar compatibility, and has identity element 1 ∈ F<X>.

3. Monomials, Degree, and Homogeneity

Monomial: a nonzero scalar multiple of a word: λ w where λ ∈ F, w ∈ X*.

Length and Degree:

  • Length of a word w = x_{i1} ··· x_{im}: ℓ(w) := m, and ℓ(1):=0.
  • Degree of a nonzero polynomial f = \sum λ_i w_i: 
    deg(f) := max{ ℓ(w_i) | λ_i ≠ 0 }.

Homogeneous polynomial: all monomials in f have the same length (degree).

Multilinear polynomial: each indeterminate appears exactly once in each monomial. Formally, 

f(x_1, …, x_n) = \sum_{σ ∈ S_n} λ_{σ} x_{σ(1)} ··· x_{σ(n)}, \quad λ_{σ} ∈ F.

4. Universal Property

Let A be any unital F-algebra and f : X → A be a function. Then there exists a unique unital algebra homomorphism 

\tilde{f} : F<X> → A
such that \tilde{f}(x) = f(x) for all x ∈ X. This universal property makes F<X> the free object in the category of unital F-algebras.

5. Evaluation

Given f(x_1, …, x_n) ∈ F<X> and elements a_1, …, a_n ∈ A, the evaluation 

f(a_1, …, a_n)
is obtained by replacing each x_i by a_i, preserving order.

Example: If f = x_1^2 x_2 x_1 − x_2 x_3 + 1, then 

f(a_1,a_2,a_3) = a_1^2 a_2 a_1 − a_2 a_3 + 1 ∈ A.

6. Domain Property

The free algebra F<X> is a domain. That is, for any nonzero f,g ∈ F<X>, 

deg(f g) = deg(f) + deg(g).
